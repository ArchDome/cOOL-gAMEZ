# Game design principles

The owner's game-making philosophy, collected from their feedback. Every session reads this before designing a mechanic. When the owner states a new tenet, add it here, in their words where possible.

## 1. Say yes to the player

Play the game in your head. Whenever a player thinks "can I do this?", the answer should be yes as often as possible. If they want to break through a wall, let them. If they punch the ground while falling, something should happen.

## 2. A very high skill ceiling, through expressive movement

- Prefer mechanics that **interact with each other** (a bat swing is also a ground bounce, the grapple can carry a thrown item, a magnet trap catches players mid-swing).
- Every movement tool should have a basic use plus advanced, discoverable tricks: bounce-cancels, momentum carry, trickshots.
- Reward style with mechanics like the 360-charged sniper: the requirement *is* the trick.

## 3. Cater to every player type, and reward what's fun for each

- **Racers** (competitive): sparks are the main score. Being first should pay off (the final spark spawns closer to the leader).
- **Fighters** (aggressive): kills and knockoffs earn points and stats, and there's a sumo-style deathmatch mode.
- **Trolls and tinkerers** (casual): map traps, throwable props, and destructible stuff to mess with people. Awards recognize them too.
- At the end of a cup, give out **custom awards** so everyone gets recognized for their style.

## 4. Maps are toys

Every map has at least one interactive element that players can trigger: traps, like those in death-run minigames (timed hazards, levers, magnets), plus set-piece events (throw the idol into the volcano and it erupts).

## 5. Make moments dramatic

Big events get a fuss: music swells, light shows, the announcer, the camera. A death gets a splash and a moment to admire it (with a skip button).

## 6. Clear competition

The HUD always shows who is winning and who is close behind. Track lots of stats (kills, deaths, sparks, falls, distance, air time) and show them.

## 6b. Pop-ups are for you

Toasts and announcer lines only fire for actions that involve the player: they hit you, you hit someone, something is coming for you, or the race itself changes. Other people's fights stay visual and audible in the world, not on your screen.

## 7. Everything is customizable

Every setting a player might want to fine-tune should be an option: sensitivity, aim assist, gyro, control scheme, HUD size, audio. Sensible defaults for each device.

## 8. Fit the device

Detect the input method (touch, mouse and keyboard, gamepad) and switch the whole experience to match: hide keyboard hints on touch, use a compact HUD on small screens, and offer a simplified control scheme (the camera follows behind, tap to hook) for phones and tablets.

## 9. Original

Original music, characters and art. No recreations of copyrighted songs or games.
